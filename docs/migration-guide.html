Right now, mail to the domain bounces

When the domain’s nameservers moved to Netlify, its mail (MX) records didn’t come across. Until the records below are added, nothing sent to an @catepates.co.nz address arrives.

The steps below are for MyHost. Other providers work the same way: they give you mail records, and you add those in Netlify, not at the provider.

1. Create the mailbox at MyHost

2. Add the mail records in Netlify

The domain’s DNS is run by Netlify, so records added at MyHost are ignored. Go to Netlify → Domains → catepates.co.nz → DNS records → Add new record and add:

TypeNameValueWhy
MX@ (leave blank)MyHost’s mail host, priority as given (usually 10)Delivers mail to the mailbox
TXT@ (leave blank)MyHost’s SPF text, e.g. v=spf1 include:… ~allSays MyHost may send for the domain, so mail isn’t marked as spam
TXTdefault._domainkey (as MyHost shows)MyHost’s DKIM keySigns outgoing mail
TXT_dmarcv=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:hello@catepates.co.nzTells Gmail and others how to treat fakes; start with p=none

Netlify subscription: where things stand

On 13 October 2026 Dom upgraded his team (“Dominic’s Team”) from the free plan to Netlify Pro, paying for one month so Cate could join as a team member and see the preview before launch. Cate was invited at catepatesnz@gmail.com. Pro renews monthly until it’s cancelled, so finish this step before about 13 November 2026, then move Dom’s team back to the free plan (Team settings → Billing). Being a member of Dom’s team already lets Cate transfer the project to her own (Option A). Her own team can start on the free plan; check its monthly build credits cover the site (each publish uses about 15).

Option A: transfer the project

Keeps everything: settings, form messages, the sold-pieces store and DNS. Netlify only lets you move a project to a team you’re a member of, and free teams have limited members, so check Netlify’s current rules first.
