The Google Sheet

The website sheet has five tabs, and the website reads all of them. Keep the tab names and the first-row headings exactly as below, or the website can’t find them. Add as many rows as you like underneath.

The Show column

On most tabs, type yes to show the row on the website or no to hide it. Use no for drafts, or to take something down without deleting it.

Prints

A print is a row in the Studio tab like any other, with Type Print and an Edition. Prints appear in their own Prints section on the Gallery page, after all the ceramics.

  • Limited edition (e.g. 25): the website counts the online sales and shows Edition of 25, then 3 left near the end, then Sold out. Sold some in person? Type how many under Sold (e.g. 4) and the website counts those too. In the Stripe payment link, also tick Limit the number of payments and enter the copies left, so Stripe itself stops selling when the edition is gone. Leave Let customers adjust quantity off.
  • Open edition (Open): never sells out. Type yes under Sold if you stop selling it.
  • Each size is its own row with its own payment link (e.g. PR-01-A4 and PR-01-A3).
  • The Sold and Edition columns must be formatted as Plain text (select both columns → Format → Number → Plain text). Otherwise Google drops a 4 in a column of yes/no, or an Open in a column of numbers.

Photos of prints go in the same Studio Photos Drive folder as the ceramics: upload, Share → Anyone with the link, copy the link into Photo link.

The Gallery page has a filter: All · Houses · Vessels · Creatures. ORA and Vault pieces are sorted by their titles (and ORA’s own tags), so there’s nothing to do for those. A Prints button appears by itself once a piece has the type Print.

Recreating the sheet from scratch

  1. Make a new Google Sheet called Fig Tree Studio website.
  2. Add the five website tabs with exactly the names above, and the headings in row 1. Dom has a template file you can import instead (File → Import).
  3. Click Share → General access → Anyone with the link → Viewer. The website needs this to read it; nobody can change it without being invited.
  4. Send Dom the link so he can point the website at it.

Adding a workshop

A workshop appears on the Workshops page as soon as its row says yes under Show, and disappears by itself the day after it has happened.

  1. Set up the booking in Humanitix. Under refunds choose Custom and paste the wording below. Add a checkout question with a tick box, I’ve read and agree to the workshop terms, and make it required if Humanitix allows. Turn on self-service refunds (100% of the ticket price, up to 7 days before). Then copy the event’s link.
  2. In the Workshops tab, add a row: date, time, name, venue, price, a short description and the booking link.
  3. Put no under Show while you’re still working on it. Change it to yes when it’s ready to go live.
  4. On the website the workshop shows a Book a place button that opens your Humanitix page, with “Tickets through Humanitix” underneath. A booking page somewhere else (a library or venue) works too: it says “Booking through” that site. With no link, the button says Ask about booking and opens the contact form with the workshop already named. Type none for drop-in sessions.
  5. When it’s full, type yes under Sold out. The button changes to “Sold out” so nobody tries to book.
  6. Leave old workshops in the sheet if you like; past dates are hidden automatically.

Refund wording to paste into Humanitix

Cancel 7 or more days before the workshop for a full refund of the ticket price or a transfer to another workshop. After that, tickets aren’t refundable or transferable, but you’re welcome to give or sell your place to someone else. If a workshop has to be cancelled, you’ll get a full refund including booking fees, or a transfer to another workshop. Your rights under the Consumer Guarantees Act aren’t affected. Full terms: catepates.co.nz/terms.html

Privacy

Keep the venue to a public place. Never put your home address in the sheet: the website shows everything in a row marked yes.

Setting up a workshop in Humanitix

Humanitix keeps count of places for you, lets people book several at once, and keeps the list of who’s coming, so there’s no tally to update by hand.

  1. Create the event: name, date and time, a public venue, a short description and a photo.
  2. Tickets: one ticket called Workshop place. Set the quantity to the number of places (for example 10) and allow up to 4 per order. When they’re gone, sales stop by themselves.
  3. Booking fee: Humanitix charges 5% + 49c per paid ticket, plus GST (about $6 on a $95 ticket). The simplest choice is to absorb the fee and set an all-inclusive price: about $101 leaves you roughly $95, and buyers see one price with no booking fee added.
  4. Refunds: choose Custom and paste the refund wording above. Turn on self-service refunds: 100% of the ticket price, up to 7 days before.
  5. Checkout questions: a tick box I’ve read and agree to the workshop terms (required, if Humanitix allows), and Anything Cate should know? (allergies, access needs).
  6. Publish, copy the event link, and paste it into the Booking link column of the Workshops tab.

While bookings come in

  • Humanitix emails you each booking. The Orders and Attendees pages show everyone, with their answers; you can print or download the list for the day.
  • Turn on the waitlist so people can join it once it’s full.
  • When it’s full, type yes under Sold out in the sheet so the website says so too.

One list for every booking

If it isn’t in Humanitix, it isn’t booked: no separate notebook or tally.

Seeing the count: the event dashboard shows places taken against places available (for example 8/10), online and manual together. The Attendees report is everyone coming, to print for the day. The Orders report shows who paid online and who paid you directly, so you can tick off bank transfers as they arrive.

A place and the money are separate

Humanitix counts people with a place. The money is either taken by Humanitix (online bookings) or paid straight to you (manual bookings).

When someone books directly

  1. They message you. Check the count in Humanitix: is there room?
  2. Send them your bank details.
  3. When the money arrives, add the manual order (Orders → + New manual order). Their place is taken and they get a ticket email.

To hold a place before they’ve paid, add the manual order straight away instead, and cancel it if the money hasn’t arrived after a few days; that frees the place again.

Check the count first

Online sales stop when it’s full, but Humanitix will let you add a manual booking past the limit (11/10). Before adding one, glance at the count; if it’s full, offer the waitlist.

Private groups (birthdays, team days): agree the date and price by message; you don’t need Humanitix for those.

Listing a piece for sale

Each piece you sell yourself needs a photo, a Stripe payment link and one row in the Studio tab. Allow about 10 minutes per piece.

1. The photo

  1. Take the photo on a plain, light background, with the piece upright, centred and filling most of the frame.
  2. Upload it to the Studio Photos folder in Google Drive.
  3. Right-click it → Share → Anyone with the link → Copy link.

2. The payment link, in Stripe

  1. Go to Payment links → New.
  2. In Product, type the piece’s name and choose + Add new product: the name, the price in NZD, and One-off. A photo is optional here: it shows on Stripe’s checkout page and the receipt, while the website uses the photo from the sheet.
  3. Set the options:
    • Quantity: fixed at 1.
    • Limit the number of payments: 1. This stops it selling twice.
    • Collect customer addresses: billing and shipping, New Zealand only, with your delivery options (for example Courier, insured and Pick-up, arranged by email).
    • Tick Require customers to accept your terms of service.
    • On the After payment tab, choose Don’t show confirmation page and redirect to https://catepates.co.nz/order-thanks.html.
    • Leave tax off (unless you register for GST) and leave payment methods as they are.
  4. Click Create link (top right; it stays greyed out until a product is added) and copy the link it gives you.

3. The row

In the Studio tab add a code (for example FOX-01), the title, price, size, photo link and payment link, and yes under Show. The piece appears on the Gallery page under From Cate’s studio, with a Buy now button.

No payment link yet? Leave it blank. The button then says Enquire and goes to the contact form.

When something sells

Stripe tells you straight away, and the website marks the piece Sold by itself within a minute.

How you’ll know

  • An email from Stripe: the piece, the amount, and the buyer’s name, email and delivery address.
  • A notification from the Stripe app on your phone, if you’ve installed it.
  • The buyer gets a receipt from Stripe and a thank-you page saying you’ll be in touch.

What to do

  1. Email the buyer to say thank you and when it’ll be sent, or to arrange pick-up.
  2. Pack it well and send it insured, using the address in the Stripe email.
  3. In the Studio tab, type yes under Sold. The site already shows it as sold; this keeps your sheet right too.
  4. Add a line to your private Sales sheet: date, code, piece, price, buyer, delivery.
  5. When it’s posted or collected, fill in Sent.
  6. Later, if you like, set Show to no to take it off the site. Leaving a few sold pieces up for a while shows your work sells.

Pick-ups

Meet somewhere public or neutral, such as a café or the gallery. Don’t give out your home address.

The Stripe link stops working after one sale, so a piece can’t be bought twice. The money arrives in your bank account a few days later, less Stripe’s fee.

Refund requests

Point people to the website’s Terms & privacy page and ask them to use the contact form, not private messages. In short: no refunds for a change of mind; a repair or refund if something arrives damaged, is faulty, or isn’t as described (that’s the law, and you can’t opt out of it). For workshops, follow the 7-day rule in the terms.

Never write “no refunds” on its own anywhere: it breaks NZ consumer law.

Galleries, exhibitions and media

These three lists work the same way: add a row, put yes under Show, and it appears on the website.

  • Galleries (About page): each gallery that stocks your work, with its street or suburb. When one stops stocking you, set Show to no.
  • Exhibitions (About page): one row per show; the newest appear first.
  • Media (Media page): an article, podcast, radio interview or video about you. The button says Read, Listen or Watch depending on the Type you choose. Newest shows first.

Messages, ORA and The Vault

Contact form messages arrive by email. Reply to the sender’s email address shown in the message. Every message is also kept in Netlify under Forms, so nothing is lost if an email goes astray.

ORA Gallery pieces appear on the Gallery page by themselves. When ORA lists a new piece of yours it shows straight away; when one sells it disappears. Nothing to do.

The Vault pieces also appear by themselves. The website checks The Vault about once an hour, so a sold piece disappears, and a new one appears, within the hour.

Money, records and tax

Stripe keeps a full record of every online sale; your Sales sheet is a simple list alongside it.

  • Payouts: Stripe pays into your bank account automatically a few days after each sale, less its fee (about 2.7% + 30c for NZ cards). See them in Stripe under Balances.
  • End of the tax year (31 March): in Stripe go to Payments → Export and download the year’s sales and fees. Give this, and your Sales sheet, to your accountant or use it for your IR3.
  • Gallery sales are separate. ORA and The Vault pay you directly and send their own statements; keep those with your records.
  • Workshop bookings through Humanitix are paid out by Humanitix, with their own report.
  • GST: you only charge it if you register. Registering becomes compulsory once your turnover goes over $60,000 in 12 months.
